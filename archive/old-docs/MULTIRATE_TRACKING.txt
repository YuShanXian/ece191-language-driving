Separating controller timing, integration and actuator response

The experiment separated control updates, motion steps and actuator filtering.
The installed SDK's effective steering rate depends on the motion step, so
reducing that step changes actuator response as well as integration precision.
A diagnostic alternative filtered once per control update and held that result
through intermediate motion steps. It was not a calibrated real actuator.

Ten saved routes, two actuator arrangements and five timing combinations gave
100 runs with zero initial steering. Forty matched-period cases reproduced the
historical implementation. Shared-time and dense checks both reported 90/100
road passes and 100/100 constant-velocity surrounding-car passes. All ten
left-turn trials failed the road check. Finer steps improved some route errors
and worsened others; they did not establish general improvement.

Evidence: config/multirate-tracking-v1.json and
runs/project-v2/multirate-tracking-v1/. Checks were discrete samples, not a
continuous swept-volume proof. No MTR retraining or selector change occurred.
